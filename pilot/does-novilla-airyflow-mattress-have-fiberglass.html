Does the Novilla AiryFlow mattress or older versions have fiberglass?

No, current Novilla AiryFlow mattresses do not contain fiberglass. Novilla states that its beds are free of fiberglass. The company removed it after a study suggested it could be a health threat. Both the Novilla AiryFlow Graphene hybrid and the AiryFlow Cooling Foam bed fall under this policy. If you're shopping for an older used bed, check the date on the tag. Novilla made this change in the past.

What fire barrier does the Novilla AiryFlow use to meet federal rules?

Novilla AiryFlow mattresses meet federal fire rules without using fiberglass. Novilla's support page confirms that its beds pass 16 CFR Part 1633 open-flame tests. They also pass 16 CFR Part 1632 smoldering tests. Part 1632 checks how a bed handles a lighted cigarette. Part 1633 measures fire size during a 30-minute open-flame test.

Novilla doesn't list the exact fiber blend of its inner fire barrier online. However, its foams are CertiPUR-US certified. They're free from PBDEs, heavy metals, phthalates, and formaldehyde. They also test under 0.5 ppm for low VOCs. Just keep in mind that CertiPUR-US only tests the foam, not the outer cover or fire liner.

How does Novilla AiryFlow compare to other Novilla mattresses?

The Novilla AiryFlow line differs from other Novilla beds in its cover and foam build. Still, all current Novilla beds are fiberglass-free. The AiryFlow Graphene hybrid pairs a knit cover with 2 to 3 inches of gel or graphene memory foam. It also uses transition foam. In contrast, the AiryFlow Cooling Foam bed is all-foam. It swaps coils for a dense foam base.

Other Novilla lines, like the Serenity, also use lab-tested foams. Those foams keep the sleep surface free of fiberglass and odors. Your choice comes down to whether you want coils or all-foam support.

What exact language appears on a Novilla AiryFlow law tag?

We haven't seen every Novilla AiryFlow law tag in person, so check the white tag sewn onto your bed. Federal rules require the build date, factory name, and compliance label. They don't require every tag to list the fire barrier fibers. Some brands list glass fiber on the filling line while others don't. It's smart to confirm with Novilla in writing too. Here's how to check your tag in four quick steps:

  1. Find the white law tag sewn into the side or head seam of the bed.
  2. Read the filling list to see if it names polyester, rayon, or foam.
  3. Check the build date and factory name, especially if you bought the bed used.
  4. Look for any mention of glass fiber or silica, which shouldn't appear on current Novilla beds.

Should you unzip or wash the Novilla AiryFlow mattress cover?

You shouldn't unzip or remove the inner fire-barrier sock on a Novilla AiryFlow mattress. The bed doesn't use fiberglass, but opening an inner fire liner can harm the flame barrier. It can also void your warranty. If your AiryFlow has an outer zip-off cover, check the care tag before washing it. Spot cleaning with mild soap and water is usually the safest way to handle spills.

Safe to do
  • Spot clean the outer cover with mild soap and a damp cloth.
  • Use a breathable waterproof mattress protector to keep spills off the cover.
  • Check the sewn-in care label before washing any removable outer layer.
Never do
  • Never unzip or remove an inner fire-barrier sock.
  • Never machine wash or tumble dry a cover unless the care tag allows it.
  • Never cut or pull loose threads from the inner fire liner.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

Has Novilla AiryFlow had any CPSC recalls or fiberglass complaints?

Novilla hasn't had a fiberglass recall, though it did have a fire-rule recall in late 2025. On Oct 30, 2025, CPSC Recall #26062 recalled about 5,340 Avenco and Novilla beds. Those beds were made by PT Champion and failed 16 CFR 1633 open-flame rules.

Affected beds have a white side label listing PT Champion and dates from Dec 2024 to Jan 9, 2025. The recalled Novilla model numbers are N-M02043-12-F-2, B-M02901-14-Q-1, and B-M02905-6-T. If you own one, stop using it and email recall@champmattress.com. You can also visit novilla.net/pages/recall for a free zippered fire cover.

How do you check an older or secondhand Novilla AiryFlow mattress?

If you're buying a used Novilla AiryFlow, check the tag and cover before bringing it home. Novilla removed fiberglass after a health study, so older beds may predate that change. Use these quick checks when you inspect a used bed:

  • Check the build date: Look at the white law tag for the build month and year. Compare it against the 2025 PT Champion recall dates.
  • Read the fiber list: Scan the filling section for any mention of glass fiber. Current Novilla beds are fiberglass-free.
  • Inspect the outer cover: Make sure the cover isn't torn, stained, or missing its zipper pull.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Novilla AiryFlow bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
