Does Avocado mattress have fiberglass?

JPBy Justine Pinto · Updated Nov 24, 2026

The 30-second answer

No, Avocado mattresses do not contain fiberglass. Avocado says plainly that no Avocado mattress contains fiberglass. It says its mattresses never contain it. Most models use certified organic wool as a natural fire barrier. The Vegan Mattress uses charcoal-infused latex or organic cotton batting. Every Avocado mattress is also GOTS-certified at the finished-product level. That certification bans fiberglass.

Avocado basics

Which Avocado models or years have fiberglass?

None of Avocado's current mattresses contain fiberglass. Avocado says no Avocado mattress contains fiberglass. It says its mattresses never contain it. That covers the Green Mattress too. Avocado says it has no fiberglass and no chemical adhesives between comfort layers.

Avocado does not list any older model that used fiberglass in the sources reviewed here. If you own an older Avocado, read the law tag on your own mattress. Do not assume.

There is more proof. Every Avocado mattress is GOTS-certified organic at the finished-product level. It holds six finished-product certifications. GOTS finished-product certification clearly bans fiberglass. An independent third-party audit checks this. The Green Mattress carries GOTS finished-product certification under license CU863637.

Avocado basics

What fire barrier does Avocado use?

Avocado uses certified organic wool as its natural flame barrier on most models. Every Avocado mattress uses certified organic wool as a natural flame barrier, except the Vegan Mattress. That wool has no additives, synthetics, or blended fibers such as rayon or polyester. It has no chemical flame retardants. The wool chars rather than burns. That is how the Green Mattress meets federal flammability standards without chemical flame retardants.

ModelFire barrierFiberglass
Green Mattress and most Avocado modelsCertified organic woolNo
Vegan MattressCharcoal-infused latex, with GOTS organic cotton batting cited in place of woolNo

Every mattress needs some fire barrier. Federal law (16 CFR Part 1633) requires an open-flame test. The CPSC says that test measures the size of the fire a mattress makes over 30 minutes. The goal is to reduce deaths and injuries from mattress fires. Makers can meet it with organic wool, chemically treated fabric, or a fiberglass sock cover. Fiberglass is the cheapest option.

Check your mattress

How can you check your Avocado law tag?

Find the law tag and read it before you remove anything. The law tag lists fiber content and fire barrier details. Federal rules require records of flame retardant treatments and flame resistant fibers used in mattress parts. Fiberglass is usually not listed on the label. You cannot see it from the outside, so the tag is a starting point, not a guarantee.

  1. Lift the mattress and find the white law tag sewn into the seam or foot end.
  2. Read the fiber content list. Look for the words fiberglass, glass fiber, or fiber glass.
  3. Look for a "Do Not Remove Cover" warning. Avocado says this is a signal to watch for.
  4. Check for a finished-product GOTS or EWG Verified mark. Avocado calls this the most reliable proof fiberglass is absent.
  5. Write down the model name and date. Then compare it to Avocado's own statements.

Check your mattress

Should you unzip or wash the mattress cover?

No, do not unzip or wash a structural mattress cover. Fiberglass only becomes a problem when the cover is removed or damaged. Then particles can get into the bedroom. It can also be released when a cover is torn, ripped, or removed and washed.

Avocado's own guidance is to watch for "Do Not Remove Cover" warnings. Leave that cover in place. Use a washable mattress protector on top instead. Spot clean the surface only. If a zipper is there for manufacturing, treat it as off limits.

This advice holds even on a fiberglass-free mattress. Keeping the cover intact protects the fire barrier, the foam, and your warranty.

Safety and buying

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass?

Stop using the mattress and keep the cover sealed until you know more. Fiberglass escapes when the cover is torn, ripped, or removed and washed. Do not vacuum the bedroom with a normal vacuum first. That can spread particles.

  • Check the law tag and any certification marks before you move the mattress.
  • Do not unzip, cut, or wash the cover while you investigate.
  • Bag the mattress in a mattress bag if you need to move it out of the room.
  • Contact the brand or retailer with your model name. Ask about the fire barrier in writing.
  • Wipe hard surfaces with a damp cloth. Wash bedding separately.

Safety and buying

How do you pick a fiberglass-free mattress?

Look for a finished-product certification and a clear fire barrier description. Avocado says a finished-product GOTS or EWG Verified certification is the most reliable proof fiberglass is absent. GOTS finished-product certification clearly bans fiberglass. An independent third-party audit checks this. EWG Verified products also cannot use fiberglass. They also cannot use chemical flame retardants, PVCs, or PFAS.

Ask what the fire barrier is made of. Wool that chars rather than burns is one natural option. Avocado uses it on most models. A brand that names its barrier and backs it with a finished-product certificate is easier to trust than one that stays vague.

Price alone is not a reliable guide. Mattresses under $400, especially foam-only models, are more likely to contain fiberglass. Avocado notes this is not a reliable rule. Compare specific models instead, such as Does Sealy mattress have fiberglass? or Does Novaform mattress have fiberglass?.

More FAQs about Avocado fiberglass

Is Avocado's vegan mattress fiberglass-free too?

Yes. Avocado says its mattresses never contain fiberglass. The Vegan Mattress is included in that statement. It skips wool and uses charcoal-infused latex as its flame barrier. GOTS-certified organic cotton batting is cited in place of wool.

Does GOTS certification really rule out fiberglass?

Yes. GOTS finished-product certification clearly bans fiberglass. An independent third-party audit verifies it. Every Avocado mattress is GOTS-certified at the finished-product level.

Why do some mattresses use fiberglass at all?

Federal law requires every mattress to pass an open-flame test. So each one needs a fire barrier of some kind. Makers can use organic wool, chemically treated fabric, or a fiberglass sock cover. Fiberglass is the cheapest option.
