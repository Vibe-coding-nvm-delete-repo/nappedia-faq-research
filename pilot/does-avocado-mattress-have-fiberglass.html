Do any Avocado mattresses use fiberglass?

No, none of Avocado's mattresses use fiberglass. Avocado states plainly that no Avocado mattress contains fiberglass and that its mattresses never contain it. Nappedia's brand spec agrees: all 10 Avocado beds are 100% fiberglass-free.

Every mattress needs a fire barrier by law. Avocado meets that rule with natural materials instead of a glass sock cover. Here is how the main models compare.

How do Avocado's organic wool and vegan fire barriers work?

Avocado's organic wool works as a natural fire barrier that chars instead of melting. Wool is a natural flame barrier, and Avocado uses no chemical flame retardants with it. When flame touches wool, the outer fibers burn and form a char layer. That char slows the fire and helps the bed pass the federal open-flame test, 16 CFR Part 1633.

Every Avocado mattress uses certified organic wool this way, except the Vegan Mattress. The wool carries GOTS organic certification, checked by an independent third-party audit.

The Avocado Vegan Mattress needs a different path because it uses no wool. It relies on charcoal-infused latex as its flame barrier. Avocado also lists GOTS-certified organic cotton batting as a natural flame barrier in place of wool. Nappedia reports the Vegan bed passes 16 CFR Part 1633 with GOTS cotton and a silica-free plant barrier.

Why does this matter? Fiberglass is the cheapest fire-barrier option, so some brands choose it. Avocado chose natural materials instead.

What exact language appears on an Avocado law tag?

An Avocado law tag lists natural fiber terms like Organic Wool, Organic Cotton, and Natural Latex, not glass fiber wording. The law tag is the small label sewn into the mattress. It shows the materials and the federal flammability statement.

On Avocado beds, expect to see natural materials named. Most models list organic wool as the fire barrier. The Vegan model lists organic cotton and latex instead. Red-flag terms to watch for on any mattress tag include "fiberglass," "glass fiber," "modacrylic," or a vague "fire barrier" with no material named. Fiberglass is often not listed on the label at all, so a missing material name is a warning sign.

Use this 4-step checklist:

  1. Find the law tag at the foot of the mattress.
  2. Read the fire barrier line and confirm it names wool, cotton, or latex.
  3. Look for a "Do Not Remove Cover" warning, which Avocado says can signal fiberglass.
  4. Check for a GOTS or EWG Verified mark, which Avocado treats as the most reliable proof fiberglass is absent.

Read the law tag first. It is the fastest way to check any bed.

Why do GOTS and GOLS certifications rule out fiberglass?

GOTS and GOLS certifications rule out fiberglass because both standards ban synthetic glass materials in certified products. GOTS finished-product certification explicitly prohibits fiberglass. That rule covers the whole finished item, not just the fabric. An independent third-party audit verifies Avocado's GOTS status.

Avocado treats a finished-product GOTS or EWG Verified certification as the most reliable proof that fiberglass is absent. EWG Verified products are also barred from fiberglass, harmful chemical flame retardants, PVCs, and PFAS. GOLS covers the latex foam inside the bed and sets organic standards for that material.

This matters because fiberglass is common in budget beds. It is the cheapest fire-barrier option for manufacturers. It also often goes unlisted on labels. A finished-product certification closes that gap. It tests the whole mattress, so a hidden glass sock cover would fail the audit.

Certifications prove the whole bed, not just one layer.

Can you unzip or wash an Avocado mattress cover?

No, you should not unzip or wash an Avocado mattress cover. Avocado's organic cotton covers are hand-tufted and made to stay on the bed. Fiberglass becomes a problem when a cover is removed or damaged, because particles can escape into the bedroom. Even though Avocado beds have no fiberglass, the cover is not designed to come off for washing.

Spot-clean stains with a damp cloth and mild soap. Let the area air dry fully. Use a breathable, washable mattress protector instead, and wash that protector on a regular schedule. A protector keeps sweat and spills off the cover without trapping heat.

If your cover is torn or damaged, contact Avocado support before doing anything else. Do not try to open the mattress or reach the inner layers.

Keep the cover on. Clean the protector, not the bed.

Have Avocado mattresses or mattress pads ever been recalled?

No Avocado mattress has ever been recalled by the U.S. CPSC. Nappedia reports zero CPSC recalls on any Avocado mattress.

One Avocado product did face a recall, and it was not a bed. CPSC Recall #24330, dated August 1, 2024, covered Avocado Organic Cotton Mattress Pad Protectors. The pads failed federal fire rules under 16 CFR Part 1632, not the mattress rule. The recall did not involve fiberglass. Owners received a free replacement.

This is a pad issue, not a mattress issue. It is still worth knowing for full transparency. If you own one of those pads, check the CPSC notice and request your replacement.

Zero mattress recalls. The 2024 action covered pads only.

QIs fiberglass listed on mattress labels?

Often it is not. Fiberglass in mattresses is typically not listed on the label. That is why Avocado suggests checking the law tag and looking for a "Do Not Remove Cover" warning. A finished-product GOTS or EWG Verified mark is stronger proof.

QWhy do mattresses need a fire barrier at all?

Federal law 16 CFR Part 1633 requires every mattress to pass an open-flame test. Every mattress must have a fire barrier of some kind to meet that rule. Brands can choose organic wool, treated fabric, or a fiberglass sock cover.

QDoes the Avocado Vegan Mattress use wool?

No, the Vegan Mattress skips wool on purpose. It uses charcoal-infused latex as its flame barrier. Avocado also lists GOTS-certified organic cotton batting as a natural flame barrier in place of wool.

QWhat happens if fiberglass escapes a mattress cover?

Fiberglass becomes a problem when the cover is removed or damaged, releasing particles into the bedroom. Those tiny glass fibers can irritate skin, eyes, and lungs. That is why covers should stay sealed and intact.

QIs the 2024 Avocado recall about fiberglass?

No. CPSC Recall #24330 covered Avocado Organic Cotton Mattress Pad Protectors, not the beds, for failing federal fire rules. The pads failed 16 CFR Part 1632, and owners got a free replacement. No Avocado mattress has a CPSC recall.

QHow can I confirm an Avocado bed is fiberglass-free before buying?

Look for a finished-product GOTS or EWG Verified certification, which Avocado treats as the most reliable proof. GOTS rules explicitly prohibit fiberglass in certified products. EWG Verified products are also barred from fiberglass.
