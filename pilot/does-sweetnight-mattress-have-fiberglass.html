What fire barrier does Sweetnight use to meet federal rules?

Sweetnight doesn't publish one exact fire-barrier fiber across every model and year. You'll need to check the sewn-in law tag on your bed. Every bed sold in the U.S. must pass federal fire safety rules. Under CPSC mattress rules, beds must resist smoldering cigarettes under 16 C.F.R. part 1632. They'll also need to pass a 30-minute open-flame test under 16 C.F.R. part 1633.

Fiberglass is spun from molten glass and plastic into thin woven sheets. Some older budget beds used these sheets as a cheap fire barrier. Today, Sweetnight explains on its fiberglass-free mattress guide how glass fibers work and why shoppers avoid them. Newer models use CertiPUR-US and OEKO-TEX certified foams instead.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Sweetnight models differ?

Third-party sellers may carry older stock with different fire barriers. SweetNight Innovations runs four brands, including SweetNight, Novilla, Avenco, and Tianmimi. When you're buying from a third-party marketplace, check the shipping date and box label.

If you buy a bed on clearance, it's possible it came from an older production run. Always inspect the permanent law tag as soon as the bed arrives. Direct orders from the brand site are more likely to ship fresh inventory.

Should you unzip or wash the Sweetnight mattress cover?

Don't ever unzip the inner fire sock on any mattress. Even if the outer knit cover has a zipper, removing it can disturb the fire barrier. Spot clean spills with mild soap and a damp cloth instead. Visit our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub for more care tips.

Safe to do
  • Use a waterproof mattress protector over the bed.
  • Spot clean small spills with mild soap and water.
  • Check the law tag date before you buy.
Never do
  • Unzip the outer cover if the tag warns you not to.
  • Machine wash or dry clean the inner fire sock.
  • Vacuum shiny dust with a standard household vacuum.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass in a Sweetnight bed?

Don't use a standard vacuum if you see shiny dust around your bed. A normal vacuum can blow tiny glass fibers into the air. Turn off the lights and shine a flashlight across the fabric to spot any sparkle.

Keep the outer cover zipped shut and seal the bed in a tight zippered encasement. Contact Sweetnight customer support with your order number and law tag date.

Skin, Eye & Breathing Safety (When to See a Doctor)

Rinse exposed skin with cold water only, not hot water or scrubbing. Hot water opens your pores and traps fibers deeper. See a doctor if you get a lingering cough, wheezing, eye pain, or a rash that won't clear after rinsing.

Step 1: Shut off fans and HVAC, close the door, and take photos

Turn off ceiling fans and central air right away so fibers don't spread into ducts. Take clear photos of the law tag and shiny dust before you touch the bed.

Step 2: Put on gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection

Wear long sleeves, nitrile gloves, a fitted N95 mask, and glasses before touching anything. That keeps fine glass fibers off your skin, eyes, and airways.

Step 3: Seal and double-bag the mattress before moving it

Don't drag an uncovered bed through hallways. Zip it inside a heavy waterproof vinyl encasement, tape the zipper shut, and double-bag it before disposal.

Step 4: Vacuum slowly with a sealed True HEPA vacuum only

Don't use a regular household vacuum, because it'll blow glass fibers right back out the exhaust. Use a sealed True HEPA vacuum on carpets, baseboards, and frames.

Step 5: Wet-wipe hard surfaces and replace HVAC air filters

Dry dusting pushes fibers back into the air. Wipe floors and nightstands with damp disposable cloths, then swap in fresh high-MERV HVAC filters.

Step 6: Wash lightly exposed clothes alone or toss heavy ones

Bag heavily coated sheets or curtains and throw them out. Rinse lightly exposed clothes outside, wash them alone on cold, and run two empty rinse cycles.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Sweetnight bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.

QAre new Sweetnight mattresses fiberglass-free?

Current Sweetnight models are fiberglass-free, though older stock may differ. Newer beds like the Twilight Hybrid carry CertiPUR-US and OEKO-TEX certifications.

QHow do I know when my Sweetnight mattress was made?

Federal rules require every mattress to have a permanent label. Check the white law tag for the exact month, year, and factory location.

QWhat is fiberglass made of?

Fiberglass is made by heating glass and plastic until molten, then spinning them into fine fibers. It uses the same raw materials as kitchen glass and window glass.

QDo Sweetnight hybrid and foam beds use the same materials?

No, hybrid beds like the Twilight use gel memory foam over 6-inch to 8-inch steel pocket coils. All-foam models like the Breeze stack gel foam over cut airflow foam.

QCan I wash my Sweetnight mattress cover?

You shouldn't unzip or machine wash the cover unless the law tag says it's safe. Spot clean spills instead so you don't disturb the inner fire barrier.

QWho makes Sweetnight mattresses?

SweetNight Innovations was founded in 2011 and makes Sweetnight beds. The company also runs Novilla, Avenco, and Tianmimi.
