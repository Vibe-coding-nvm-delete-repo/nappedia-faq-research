What fire barrier does Croft use to meet federal rules?

Croft uses natural fibers for fire safety instead of fiberglass. The brand states its fire barrier materials meet all safety rules. Under federal law, CPSC mattress rules aim to cut down fire deaths and injuries.

Every mattress sold in the U.S. must pass two main tests. First, 16 C.F.R. part 1632 tests how a mattress handles a lit cigarette. Second, 16 C.F.R. part 1633 measures fire size during a 30-minute open-flame test. Croft also adds a silver fiber lining under the top and side fabric.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Croft models differ?

Croft handcrafts its mattresses in Webb City, Missouri using American-made materials. All Croft builds follow the same fiberglass-free standard. If you buy from a third-party seller, always check the sewn-in law tag first. That tag confirms the exact materials inside your specific bed.

What exact language appears on a Croft law tag?

On a Croft law tag, look for Natural Fibers or CertiPUR-US Certified Foam and make sure glass fiber or fiberglass is not listed. Federal rules require a permanent label on every mattress. We have not photographed every individual Croft tag in-house. However, federal law requires the tag to list the manufacture month, year, and factory location.

Follow these 4 steps to check your Croft law tag: 1. Find the white sewn-in tag at the head or foot of the bed. 2. Check the manufacture month, year, and Webb City, Missouri factory details. 3. Read the filling materials list to confirm natural fibers and CertiPUR-US foams. 4. Look for the letter T if a pad has chemical flame retardants.

Should you unzip or wash the Croft mattress cover?

Never unzip or remove a mattress cover unless the care tag says it is washable. Croft places a silver fiber lining just under the fabric on the top and sides. Opening the cover can damage the fire barrier or void your protection. Also, Croft's limited warranty does not cover stains, liquid damage, or misuse.

Safe to do
  • Use a waterproof mattress protector to prevent stains and liquid damage.
  • Spot clean the outer fabric gently with mild soap and water.
  • Vacuum the mattress surface lightly using a clean upholstery attachment.
Never do
  • Never unzip the outer cover to wash it in a machine.
  • Never pull or cut the silver fiber lining under the fabric.
  • Never soak the mattress, as liquid damage voids the warranty.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab the spot lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild dish soap. Never soak the fabric or scrub through the top quilt into the fire-barrier layer underneath.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Leave the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until fully dry. Machine-washing or tumble-drying a structural mattress cover can shrink it and expose the inner barrier.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the intact cover so sweat, spills, and dust mites never reach the mattress fabric in the first place.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Croft mattress? Here is what each safety term on a mattress tag or product page actually means.

Inner Fire Sock (FR Sock)

A thin, stretchy mesh sleeve pulled tightly around the foam core underneath the outer cover. It slows open flames, and can shed tiny glass shards if the outer cover is unzipped or torn.

Assembly Zipper vs. Washable Cover

A zipper on the bottom of a mattress usually exists only for factory assembly, not washing. Never unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is removable and washable.

Glass Fiber / Core-Spun Glass

Common U.S. law-tag names for fiberglass. Treat 'glass fiber,' 'fiberglass,' 'glass wool,' 'core-spun glass,' and 'textured glass yarn' as the same woven glass barrier.

Silica-Treated Rayon vs. Fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon or viscose chars safely like ash and does not shed sharp glass needles. Seeing 'silica-treated rayon' alone is not the same as woven glass fiber.

CertiPUR-US Does Not Mean Fiberglass-Free

CertiPUR-US only tests the foam inside the bed for VOCs and chemicals, never the outer cover or fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-Sided Encasement vs. Fitted Protector

A fitted protector only covers the top and sides, leaving the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you want to seal a suspect mattress completely.

URN & Manufacture Date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date on the white law tag identify the exact factory and year. Use that date to check pre-transition or older stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Two federal fire rules every U.S. bed must pass: 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame (which pushed budget foam brands to use fire socks).
