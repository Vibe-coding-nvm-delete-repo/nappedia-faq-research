What fire barrier does Arizona Premium Mattress use to meet federal rules?

Arizona Premium Mattress uses Pure Joma Wool and rayon fire barriers to pass federal flame tests. Under 16 C.F.R. part 1633, every U.S. bed must resist an open flame for 30 minutes. Under 16 C.F.R. part 1632, beds must also resist smoldering from a lit cigarette.

On its Solid Core 7-inch Latex Foam Mattress, the brand quilts 1.5 inches of Pure Joma Wool into each side. That wool sits inside a bamboo stretch knit cover. Its Ultra Plush Talalay bed uses the same 1.5-inch wool quilt on both sides. For its Organic Cotton Zippered Covers, the brand pairs organic cotton with a rayon fire barrier layer.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Arizona Premium Mattress models differ?

Arizona Premium Mattress sells mainly through its own site, Mattresses.net, not big-box chains like Amazon or Costco. Because you buy direct from the maker, you don't need to worry about secret store-only builds. Still, Mattresses.net doesn't publish GOTS, GOLS, or GREENGUARD finished-bed certificates. It also lacks a written fiberglass-free statement for every budget RV and poly model. If you're looking at a clearance, custom, or budget poly bed, ask customer service in writing before you order.

What exact language appears on an Arizona Premium Mattress law tag?

You'll see fiber terms like wool, cotton, rayon, or latex on an Arizona Premium Mattress law tag. We haven't photographed every tag in-house, though. Federal rules require the build date, factory site, and compliance label on the tag. They don't require every brand to list the fire barrier or cover fibers. Some makers list glass fiber on the filling line while others don't, so confirm with the maker in writing too.

  1. Check the white sewn-in law tag at the head or foot of your bed.
  2. Read the "All New Material" filling lines for terms like "Wool" or "Rayon".
  3. Look for "Glass Fiber," "Fiberglass," or "Modacrylic" as warning terms.
  4. Email or call Mattresses.net with your model name if the tag doesn't name the fire barrier.

Should you unzip or wash the Arizona Premium Mattress cover?

You shouldn't machine-wash or remove a fire-barrier cover unless the care label says it's safe. Arizona Premium Mattress uses zippered bamboo, wool, and organic cotton covers on many beds. Those zippers let you access or adjust the latex layers inside. Even on a zippered bed, you must keep the wool or rayon fire-barrier layer intact. That keeps your bed safe and meets federal flame rules. Spot-clean the outer fabric with mild soap and water instead of using a washer.

Safe to do
  • Spot-clean the outer cover gently with mild soap and water.
  • Use a breathable waterproof mattress protector to keep the wool or cotton clean.
  • Check the care label before unzipping any outer cover to adjust latex layers.
Never do
  • Never machine-wash or tumble-dry a wool-quilted or rayon fire-barrier cover.
  • Never remove or cut away the inner fire-barrier layer.
  • Never use bleach or harsh cleaners on the organic cotton or bamboo fabric.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

How do you check an older or secondhand Arizona Premium Mattress?

You can check an older or used Arizona Premium Mattress bed before you bring it home. Inspect the law tag, zipper, and outer fabric first. Even though the brand's core latex lines are fiberglass-free, used beds can have swapped covers or worn seams.

  • Check the build date on the sewn-in law tag to confirm the bed's age.
  • Read the fiber list for "Wool," "Cotton," or "Rayon," and skip any tag that lists "Glass Fiber".
  • Check the outer cover and zipper for tears, fraying, or missing inner layers.
  • Shine a flashlight along the seams in a dark room to spot any shiny fibers poking through.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Arizona Premium Mattress bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
