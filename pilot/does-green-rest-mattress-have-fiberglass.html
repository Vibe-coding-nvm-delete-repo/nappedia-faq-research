What fire barrier does Green Rest use to meet federal rules?

Green Rest uses fiberglass-free barriers to keep glass fibers from escaping. All U.S. mattresses must pass strict federal fire rules. Under CPSC safety rules, 16 C.F.R. part 1633 tests open-flame fire size for 30 minutes. Part 1632 tests if a bed resists smoldering from a lit cigarette.

Green Rest builds use natural latex comfort layers and firm-support cores alongside their fiberglass-free barriers. However, GOTS organic cotton and GOLS organic latex certificates are not published on their product pages.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Green Rest models differ?

Green Rest states all builds are fiberglass-free, no matter where you buy them. Still, third-party sellers or older stock can sometimes have different tags. Before you buy, ask Green Rest in writing to confirm your exact model uses a fiberglass-free fire barrier.

Also check the return rules for your seller. For example, Green Rest requires a bed to be clean, undamaged, and have the law tag attached for returns.

What exact language appears on a Green Rest law tag?

Federal rules require a permanent law tag on every mattress. The tag must list the month, year, and factory location of manufacture. On a Green Rest bed, the sewn-in law tag lists its Orlando, U.S. build date and natural latex fill. We have not photographed every individual tag in-house, so always check yours.

  1. Find the white sewn-in law tag on the mattress seam.
  2. Check for the Orlando, U.S. factory location and build date.
  3. Read the fill list to confirm natural latex and fiberglass-free materials.
  4. Keep the law tag attached, as Green Rest requires it for returns.

Should you unzip or wash the Green Rest mattress cover?

Never remove or cut the inner fire barrier on any mattress. Green Rest states all builds are fiberglass-free, which prevents glass fibers from escaping through zippered or worn covers. Even so, keeping the outer cover intact protects the bed and keeps your return rights safe.

Check our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub for more tips on safe cover care.

Safe to do
  • Spot clean the outer cover gently with mild soap and water.
  • Use a breathable mattress protector to keep the bed clean and undamaged.
  • Leave the sewn-in law tag attached to keep your return rights.
Never do
  • Never remove or cut the sewn-in law tag.
  • Never unzip or open an inner fire-barrier sock.
  • Never machine-wash a mattress cover unless the tag clearly allows it.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab the spot lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild dish soap. Never soak the fabric or scrub through the top quilt into the fire-barrier layer underneath.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Leave the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until fully dry. Machine-washing or tumble-drying a structural mattress cover can shrink it and expose the inner barrier.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the intact cover so sweat, spills, and dust mites never reach the mattress fabric in the first place.

How do you check an older or secondhand Green Rest mattress?

Check the sewn-in law tag first if you buy a used Green Rest bed. Look for these three things:

  • Build date and place: Confirm the month, year, and Orlando, U.S. factory location on the tag.
  • Fiber list: Check that the tag lists natural latex fill and no glass fiber.
  • Intact cover: Make sure the outer cover is clean, undamaged, and has no tears.
Current models are fiberglass-free. For general fiberglass cleanup steps on older secondhand beds, see our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub.
