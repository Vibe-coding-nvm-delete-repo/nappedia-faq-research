Does Sealy have fiberglass? Sealy says no. See the fire barriers it uses, what to check on the law tag, and safe steps if you suspect fibers.

Which Sealy models or years have fiberglass?

No current Sealy model is listed as containing fiberglass. Sealy states plainly that its mattresses do not contain any form of fiberglass. Cocoon by Sealy says the same about its beds.

What fire barrier does Sealy use to meet federal rules?

Sealy uses a rayon and polyester blend as the flame barrier in every Sealy mattress. That blend lets the bed pass fire-resistance testing and meet federal, state, and local fire rules, including the National Flammability Standard.

Cocoon by Sealy uses the same rayon and polyester blend. Sealy also says Cocoon models and hybrid mattresses are made without chemical flame retardants.

For all other foam-only models, Tempur Sealy says the bed uses core-spun glass fiber technology. In this build, amorphous glass is wrapped in a flexible protective sheath. Sealy says this glass fiber sits only in the protective fire sock, never in the foam or the mattress cover. The company also says it stays fully enclosed and out of reach during normal use, even if the cover comes off.

Federal rule 16 CFR Part 1633 requires beds to resist open flames. The CPSC says this rule tests the fire size a mattress set gives off during a 30-minute burn. Nappedia reports that on newer Sealy law tags, rayon is often listed with silica. Silica-treated rayon chars to block air instead of melting or shedding glass dust when heated.

What exact language appears on a Sealy law tag?

The law tag is the white label sewn into the mattress seam, and it lists the actual materials in that bed. Look for these terms: Rayon, Polyester, Glass Fiber, and Silica. Rayon and polyester point to the standard blend barrier. Silica often appears next to rayon on newer tags. Glass Fiber may appear on some foam-only builds, where Sealy says it is sealed inside the fire sock.

We have not photographed every Sealy law tag in-house, so treat this as a guide, not a full list. Tags can change by model, plant, and year.

Use this 4-step check before you buy:

  1. Find the white law tag sewn into the seam.
  2. Read the fiber content line and note every material listed.
  3. Write down the model number and plant code printed on the tag.
  4. Call Sealy support with those numbers if any term is unclear.

Do Costco, Amazon, or Mattress Firm Sealy models differ?

Yes, retailer-exclusive Sealy beds can carry different model names and different law tags than beds sold on Sealy.com. Stores like Costco, Amazon, and Mattress Firm often sell renamed builds made for that store. The fire barrier inside can also differ by model and year.

This does not mean those beds contain fiberglass. Sealy says its mattresses do not contain any form of fiberglass. It does mean you should not assume a store model matches a Sealy.com listing.

Always read the law tag on the exact floor unit or box you plan to take home. If you are sensitive to chemicals, or you are buying a floor model, call Sealy support with the model number and plant code from the tag. Sealy also says all its products comply with CPSC rules and California Proposition 65.

Should you unzip or wash a Sealy mattress cover?

No. Never unzip or wash a Sealy cover, because doing so can disturb the fire sock and void the warranty. The fire sock is the inner layer that holds the flame barrier in place. Sealy says the core-spun glass fiber stays protected even if the cover is removed, but the sock itself should still stay sealed.

Keep the cover zipped and spot clean only. If the cover is damaged, contact Sealy support before you use the bed. For more on barrier safety, see our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub.

Safe to do
  • Keep the cover zipped and spot clean stains with a damp cloth.
  • Use a mattress protector to keep the cover clean.
  • Call Sealy support with your law tag numbers if you have questions.
Never do
  • Never unzip or remove a structural cover or inner fire sock.
  • Never machine wash or dry the mattress cover.
  • Never vacuum the bed with a non-HEPA vacuum if you suspect loose fibers.

Has Sealy had any fiberglass lawsuits or CPSC recalls?

No. A CPSC SaferProducts check on November 22, 2026 found zero active or past mattress recalls for Sealy. Nappedia also found 0 mattress recalls for Sealy or Posturepedic beds in the same database.

We found no verified fiberglass lawsuit against Sealy in the facts we reviewed, so we do not report one. On the business side, parent Tempur Sealy beat an FTC suit in 2025 to buy Mattress Firm. That case was about competition and mergers, not fiberglass.

Sealy says all its products comply with applicable laws and regulations, including CPSC rules and California Proposition 65. You can check recalls yourself at the CPSC mattress page.

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass in a Sealy bed?

Stop vacuuming the bed with a non-HEPA vacuum right away, because it can spread fine particles into the air. Then contain the bed and call Sealy support with your law tag numbers.

  • Stop the vacuum. Do not use a standard vacuum on the mattress or floor near it. Use a HEPA vacuum or a damp cloth instead.
  • Seal the bed. Put a zippered encasement over the mattress so any loose material stays inside.
  • Wash fabrics apart. Wash exposed sheets and blankets separately from other laundry, and rinse the machine after.
  • Call Sealy support. Give them the model number and plant code from the law tag.
  • Keep the cover zipped. Do not open the cover or fire sock, since that can void the warranty.

Most Sealy beds use a rayon and polyester barrier, not loose fiberglass. If your bed is a foam-only model, Sealy says the glass fiber stays sealed in the fire sock.

What are the best verified fiberglass-free alternatives to Sealy?

Prioritize a barrier you can confirm on the law tag, such as wool, thistle, or a rayon-only blend. These materials char and block air instead of shedding glass dust. Ask the brand for the exact barrier before you buy, and check the tag when the bed arrives.

QDoes Sealy use fiberglass in its mattresses?

Sealy says its mattresses do not contain any form of fiberglass. The company says the flame barrier in every Sealy mattress is a rayon and polyester blend. Cocoon by Sealy makes the same fiberglass-free statement.

QWhat is core-spun glass fiber in a Sealy mattress?

It is a fire-barrier material used in some foam-only Sealy models. Sealy says the glass is wrapped in a protective sheath and sits only in the fire sock, never in the foam or cover. The company says it stays fully enclosed during normal use.

QCan fiberglass leak out of a Sealy mattress?

Sealy says the core-spun glass fiber stays fully enclosed and out of reach, even if the cover is removed. Most Sealy beds use a rayon and polyester barrier instead, which does not contain glass. Do not unzip the cover or fire sock, since that can void the warranty.

QDo Cocoon by Sealy mattresses contain fiberglass?

No. Cocoon by Sealy says its mattresses do not contain any form of fiberglass. The flame barrier is a rayon and polyester blend. Sealy also says Cocoon models and hybrids are made without chemical flame retardants.

QWhy does my new Sealy or Cocoon bed smell like foam?

A mild new-bed smell is normal, and boxed Cocoon beds can smell like new foam for 24 to 48 hours. Sealy foams are CertiPUR-US certified and meet federal fire rules. Air out the room and the smell should fade.

QHow do I check a Sealy law tag for fiberglass terms?

Find the white law tag sewn into the seam and read the fiber content line. Look for Rayon, Polyester, Glass Fiber, and Silica. If anything is unclear, call Sealy support with the model number and plant code from the tag.
