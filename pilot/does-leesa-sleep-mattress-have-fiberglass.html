Which Leesa Sleep models or years have fiberglass?

Current Leesa Sleep beds do not have fiberglass, though pre-2023 beds did. In 2023, Leesa moved its whole factory to fiberglass-free builds. Older Original and Studio beds made before 2023 used inner fiberglass socks.

What fire barrier does Leesa Sleep use to meet federal rules?

Current Leesa Sleep beds use a chemical-free rayon fire-retardant yarn instead of glass fibers. This rayon fiber comes from 100% regenerated cellulose. Natural models also use natural wool and safe threads.

These parts help Leesa pass federal fire rules. Every bed meets 16 CFR Part 1632 for cigarette tests and 16 CFR Part 1633 for open-flame tests. All current Leesa beds also hold GREENGUARD Gold Certification after tests for over 360 VOCs.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Leesa Sleep models differ?

Third-party sellers may still hold older stock made before the 2023 switch. Leesa's current factory is fiberglass-free. Still, clearance beds from Amazon or other sellers could be pre-2023 Original or Studio models.

Check the made date and fiber list on the white law tag right after delivery. If you buy direct from Leesa's official support, you get current fiberglass-free builds.

What exact language appears on a Leesa Sleep law tag?

We have not taken photos of every Leesa Sleep law tag in-house. Still, U.S. law says the white tag must list all inner and outer fibers by weight.

  1. Find the white law tag sewn into the seam at the foot or side.
  2. Check the made date to confirm the bed was built in 2023 or later.
  3. Look for safe barrier terms like rayon, cellulose, or wool.
  4. Watch for glass fiber terms on any pre-2023 Original or Studio bed.

Should you unzip or wash the Leesa Sleep mattress cover?

Never unzip or machine-wash the outer cover on any Leesa Sleep bed. This rule applies even to new 2026 fiberglass-free models. Taking off or washing the cover can harm the fire barrier and void your warranty.

On pre-2023 beds, unzipping the cover can also disturb the inner fiberglass sock. Spot-clean spills gently and use a washable bed protector instead.

Safe to do
  • Keep a washable waterproof protector on the bed.
  • Spot-clean small spills with mild soap and cold water.
  • Leave the outer cover zipped and intact at all times.
Never do
  • Never unzip the outer cover on any Leesa model.
  • Never put the mattress cover in a washing machine.
  • Never use a standard vacuum if you suspect fiberglass.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab the spot lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild dish soap. Never soak the fabric or scrub through the top quilt into the fire-barrier layer underneath.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Leave the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until fully dry. Machine-washing or tumble-drying a structural mattress cover can shrink it and expose the inner barrier.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the intact cover so sweat, spills, and dust mites never reach the mattress fabric in the first place.

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass in a Leesa Sleep bed?

If you own a pre-2023 Leesa bed and see shiny fibers, do not use a standard vacuum. A regular vacuum can blow tiny glass shards into the air and spread them.

  1. Shine a flashlight along the seams and floor in a dark room to spot glinting fibers.
  2. Seal the mattress right away inside a heavy-duty zippered cover.
  3. Contact Leesa Sleep support with your law tag photo and order number for help.
Skin, Eye & Breathing Safety (When to See a Doctor)

Rinse exposed skin with cold water only, never hot water or scrubbing (hot water opens pores and traps fibers deeper). Seek medical care if you develop persistent coughing, wheezing, shortness of breath, eye pain, or a skin rash that does not clear after rinsing.

Step 1: Shut off fans and HVAC, close the door, and photograph proof

Turn off ceiling fans and central air right away so glass shards do not spread into ducts. Before touching the bed, take clear photos of the law tag and shiny dust for warranty support or a CPSC report.

Step 2: Put on disposable gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection

Wear long sleeves, nitrile gloves, a fitted N95 respirator, and wraparound glasses before touching anything so fine fibers do not irritate your skin, eyes, or airways.

Step 3: Seal and double-bag the mattress before moving or disposing of it

Never drag an uncovered bed through hallways. Zip it inside a heavy waterproof vinyl encasement, tape the zipper seam shut, and double-bag in a heavy mattress disposal bag before hauling it to municipal waste.

Step 4: Vacuum slowly with a sealed True HEPA vacuum only

Never use a regular household vacuum, which blows glass fibers back through the exhaust. Use a sealed True HEPA vacuum on carpets, baseboards, bed frames, and soft furniture.

Step 5: Wet-wipe hard surfaces and replace HVAC air filters

Dry dusting pushes fibers back into the air. Wipe floors, nightstands, and windowsills with damp disposable microfiber cloths, then install fresh high-MERV HVAC filters.

Step 6: Wash lightly exposed clothes separately or discard heavily coated fabrics

Bag heavily coated sheets or curtains in sealed trash bags and discard them. For lightly exposed clothes, rinse outside first, wash alone on cold, and run two empty rinse cycles afterward.

QAre all new Leesa Sleep mattresses fiberglass-free?

Yes, all current Leesa beds made in 2023 or later are 100% fiberglass-free. Leesa's entire factory is fiberglass-free today.

QDid older Leesa mattresses ever use fiberglass?

Yes, pre-2023 Leesa Original and Studio beds used inner fiberglass socks. Leesa phased out fiberglass across its lineup starting in 2023.

QWhat fire barrier does Leesa use instead of fiberglass?

Leesa uses a chemical-free rayon fire-retardant yarn made from 100% regenerated cellulose. Natural models also use wool and safe threads to meet federal 16 CFR Part 1633 rules.

QCan I wash the cover on my Leesa mattress?

No, never unzip or machine-wash the outer cover, even on 2026 fiberglass-free models. Doing so harms the fire barrier and voids your Leesa warranty.

QWhat materials are in the Leesa Sapira Hybrid and Legend Hybrid covers?

The Sapira Hybrid Cool-Knit Cover is 74% Polyester, 13% Rayon, 9% Viscose, and 4% Poly/Lycra. The Legend Hybrid cover blends Polyester, Recycled Polyester, Organic Cotton, Merino Wool, Rayon, and Spandex.

QHas Leesa Sleep had any CPSC recalls?

No, a check of the U.S. CPSC recall database on 2026-11-21 found zero recalls on any Leesa bed, crib bed, or base. Leesa has zero safety recalls or FTC fines.
