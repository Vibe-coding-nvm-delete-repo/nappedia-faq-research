Which Nectar Sleep models or years have fiberglass?

Both the Nectar Classic and the Nectar Kids mattress are 100% fiberglass-free. However, buyers sued over fiberglass socks in Nectar beds sold from October 1, 2017 to June 30, 2024.

What fire barrier does Nectar Sleep use to meet federal rules?

New Nectar beds pass fire tests without using any fiberglass. Under 16 C.F.R. part 1633, every mattress must pass a 30-minute open-flame fire test. Mattresses must also resist smoldering cigarettes under 16 C.F.R. part 1632.

To meet these rules, Nectar Classic uses gel memory foam over a firm foam layer. It's paired with either base foam or 8-inch coils. Both the Nectar Classic and Nectar Kids models use CertiPUR-US certified foams. You can check our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub to see how safe fire barriers work.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Nectar Sleep models differ?

Third-party or clearance stock can differ from new beds sold directly on NectarSleep.com. New 2024 to 2026 Nectar beds on the main site don't contain fiberglass. Still, older stock can sit in warehouses for a while.

Beds sold from October 1, 2017 to June 30, 2024 were part of a lawsuit over fiberglass socks. If you're buying a used, clearance, or third-party Nectar bed, don't skip the law tag. Always check the manufacture date before you buy.

What exact language appears on a Nectar Sleep law tag?

Check the fiber line for watch-out terms like Glass Fiber / Fiberglass or Date from 2017 to 2024. You'll want to see safe terms like CertiPUR-US Certified or Date after late 2023 instead. Federal 16 CFR Part 1633 requires a permanent tag with the build date, factory location, and compliance label. Some brands list glass fiber on the filling line, while others don't. Should you unzip or wash the Nectar Sleep mattress cover?

Only wash covers marked removable, and don't ever open an inner fire sock. The Nectar Kids Memory Foam Mattress has a removable, machine-washable cover with OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certification.

On older Nectar beds made between 2017 and 2024, unzipping the cover could disturb an inner fiberglass sock. You'll want to check your model and law tag before you unzip any cover.

Safe to do
  • Wash the removable Nectar Kids outer cover as directed.
  • Check your law tag date before unzipping any Nectar cover.
  • Use a waterproof mattress protector to keep the cover clean.
Never do
  • Don't ever unzip an inner fire-barrier sock on any mattress.
  • Don't wash a cover that isn't marked machine-washable.
  • Don't ignore CPSC Recall #22740 on older Nectar Premier beds.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

Has Nectar Sleep had any CPSC recalls or fiberglass complaints?

Nectar Sleep has faced one recall and a major fiberglass lawsuit. On April 21, 2022, CPSC Recall #22740 recalled select Nectar Premier mattresses made by Elite Comfort Solutions. Those beds failed federal 16 CFR Part 1633 open-flame fire rules.

Nectar's parent company Resident Home also settled a $9M fiberglass class action lawsuit. Buyers in Todd v. Ashley Furniture Industries, LLC / Resident Home sued over fiberglass socks in Nectar, DreamCloud, and Siena beds. Those beds were sold from October 1, 2017 to June 30, 2024. The settlement offers store vouchers with a claim deadline of July 17, 2026 and no admission of fault.

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass in a Nectar Sleep bed?

Don't vacuum suspected fiberglass with a standard home vacuum, because it'll blow tiny shards into the air. Turn off your lights and shine a bright flashlight across the mattress and floor to look for shiny fibers.

Zip a tight, waterproof mattress encasement around the bed right away to trap any loose fibers. Then contact Nectar Sleep support with your law tag date and order details.

Skin, Eye & Breathing Safety (When to See a Doctor)

Rinse exposed skin with cold water only, not hot water or scrubbing. Hot water opens your pores and traps fibers deeper. See a doctor if you get a lingering cough, wheezing, eye pain, or a rash that won't clear after rinsing.

Spotted a fiberglass leak? File a public safety report at U.S. CPSC SaferProducts.gov →.

Step 1: Shut off fans and HVAC, close the door, and take photos

Turn off ceiling fans and central air right away so fibers don't spread into ducts. Take clear photos of the law tag and shiny dust before you touch the bed.

Step 2: Put on gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection

Wear long sleeves, nitrile gloves, a fitted N95 mask, and glasses before touching anything. That keeps fine glass fibers off your skin, eyes, and airways.

Step 3: Seal and double-bag the mattress before moving it

Don't drag an uncovered bed through hallways. Zip it inside a heavy waterproof vinyl encasement, tape the zipper shut, and double-bag it before disposal.

Step 4: Vacuum slowly with a sealed True HEPA vacuum only

Don't use a regular household vacuum, because it'll blow glass fibers right back out the exhaust. Use a sealed True HEPA vacuum on carpets, baseboards, and frames.

Step 5: Wet-wipe hard surfaces and replace HVAC air filters

Dry dusting pushes fibers back into the air. Wipe floors and nightstands with damp disposable cloths, then swap in fresh high-MERV HVAC filters.

Step 6: Wash lightly exposed clothes alone or toss heavy ones

Bag heavily coated sheets or curtains and throw them out. Rinse lightly exposed clothes outside, wash them alone on cold, and run two empty rinse cycles.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Nectar Sleep bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
