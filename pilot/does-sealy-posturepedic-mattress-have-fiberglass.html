What fire barrier does Sealy Posturepedic use?

Sealy Posturepedic uses a rayon and polyester flame barrier. Sealy states the flame barrier used in every Sealy mattress is a rayon and polyester blend. This barrier lets the mattress pass fire-resistance testing and meet federal, state, and local fire rules, including the National Flammability Standard.

Federal rules set the test. CPSC says 16 CFR part 1633 tests the size of the fire a mattress generates during a 30-minute open-flame test. A second rule, 16 CFR part 1632, tests resistance to a lighted cigarette.

Sealy also says hybrid mattresses and Cocoon by Sealy models are made without chemical flame retardants. Nappedia reports that newer Sealy law tags often list rayon with silica. When heated, silica-treated rayon chars to block air instead of melting or shedding glass dust.

How does Posturepedic differ from budget Sealy boxed foam beds?

Posturepedic beds use a rayon and polyester barrier, while some other foam-only beds use a glass fiber sock. Posturepedic innerspring and hybrid models carry the brand-wide rayon and polyester flame barrier. Sealy says those mattresses contain no fiberglass.

For all other foam-only models, Tempur Sealy says its mattresses use a core-spun glass fiber technology. In that design, amorphous glass is encased in a flexible protective sheath. Tempur Sealy states the glass fiber sits only in the protective fire sock, never in the foam or the cover. It stays fully enclosed and out of reach during normal use, even if the cover is removed.

So the build type matters. A traditional or hybrid Posturepedic is not the same as a foam-only boxed bed. Check the law tag to see which build you have.

What exact language appears on a Sealy Posturepedic law tag?

The law tag on a Sealy Posturepedic should list rayon and polyester, not fiberglass. Sealy says its flame barrier is a rayon and polyester blend. Nappedia reports that newer Sealy law tags often list rayon with silica.

Use this 4-step check before you buy:

  1. Find the white law tag sewn into the seam.
  2. Look for the words rayon, polyester, and sometimes silica.
  3. Scan for the word fiberglass. Sealy says its mattresses contain none.
  4. Note the month and year of manufacture, which CPSC requires on every mattress label.

Federal rules also require records of flame retardant treatments and flame resistant fibers used in mattress parts. That paper trail helps confirm what is inside the bed.

Should you unzip or wash a Sealy Posturepedic cover?

No, you should not unzip or wash a Sealy Posturepedic cover. Nappedia advises never to unzip or wash a Sealy cover, because doing so can disturb the fire sock and void the warranty. The fire sock is the protective layer that holds the flame barrier in place.

Spot clean only, and keep the cover zipped. If the cover gets dirty, blot it with a damp cloth and mild soap, then let it air dry. For deeper cleaning, contact Sealy support or a professional cleaner. Read our full Sealy brand review for more care tips.

Safe to do
  • Spot clean stains with a damp cloth and mild soap.
  • Keep the cover zipped and the fire sock sealed.
  • Air dry the cover fully after any spot cleaning.
Never do
  • Never unzip the cover to look inside the fire sock.
  • Never machine wash or tumble dry the cover.
  • Never remove the fire sock or cut into the mattress.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab the spot lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild dish soap. Never soak the fabric or scrub through the top quilt into the fire-barrier layer underneath.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Leave the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until fully dry. Machine-washing or tumble-drying a structural mattress cover can shrink it and expose the inner barrier.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the intact cover so sweat, spills, and dust mites never reach the mattress fabric in the first place.

Has Sealy Posturepedic had any CPSC recalls or fiberglass complaints?

No, Sealy Posturepedic has no active or past CPSC mattress recalls. Nappedia states a CPSC SaferProducts check on November 22, 2026 found zero active or past mattress recalls for Sealy or Posturepedic beds.

Most forum confusion comes from older beds or other brands. Specs can vary on older clearance beds. Checking your manufacture year on the law tag clears up most of it, since CPSC requires the month and year of manufacture on every mattress label.

Sealy also says all its products comply with applicable laws and rules, including those set by the Consumer Product Safety Commission and California Proposition 65. Sealy foams are CertiPUR-US certified and meet federal fire rules. For more context, visit the Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub.
