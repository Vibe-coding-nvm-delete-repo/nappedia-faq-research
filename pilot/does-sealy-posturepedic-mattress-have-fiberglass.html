What fire barrier does Sealy Posturepedic use?

Sealy Posturepedic uses a rayon and polyester flame barrier. Sealy says the flame barrier in every Sealy mattress is a rayon and polyester blend. It's what lets the mattress pass fire-resistance testing and meet federal, state, and local fire rules, including the National Flammability Standard.

Federal rules set the test. CPSC says 16 CFR part 1633 tests the size of the fire a mattress generates during a 30-minute open-flame test. A second rule, 16 CFR part 1632, tests resistance to a lighted cigarette.

Sealy also says hybrid mattresses and Cocoon by Sealy models are made without chemical flame retardants. Nappedia reports that newer Sealy law tags often list rayon with silica. When heated, silica-treated rayon chars to block air. It doesn't melt or shed glass dust.

How does Posturepedic differ from budget Sealy boxed foam beds?

Posturepedic beds use a rayon and polyester barrier, while some other foam-only beds use a glass fiber sock. Posturepedic innerspring and hybrid models carry the brand-wide rayon and polyester flame barrier. Sealy says those mattresses don't contain fiberglass.

For all other foam-only models, Tempur Sealy says its mattresses use a core-spun glass fiber technology. In that design, amorphous glass is encased in a flexible protective sheath. Tempur Sealy says the glass fiber sits only in the protective fire sock. It's never in the foam or the cover. It stays fully enclosed and out of reach during normal use, even if you remove the cover.

So your build type matters. A traditional or hybrid Posturepedic isn't the same as a foam-only boxed bed. Check your law tag to see which build you have.

What exact language appears on a Sealy Posturepedic law tag?

You'll usually see rayon and polyester on a Sealy Posturepedic law tag, not fiberglass. Sealy says its flame barrier is a rayon and polyester blend. Nappedia reports that newer Sealy law tags often list rayon with silica.

Use this 4-step check before you buy:

  1. Find the white law tag sewn into the seam.
  2. Look for the words rayon, polyester, and sometimes silica.
  3. Scan for the word fiberglass. Sealy says its mattresses don't contain any.
  4. Note the month and year of manufacture, which CPSC requires on every mattress label.

Some brands list glass fiber on the filling line while others don't, so it's smart to confirm with the maker in writing too. Federal rules also require records of flame retardant treatments and flame resistant fibers used in mattress parts. That paper trail helps confirm what's inside your bed.

Should you unzip or wash a Sealy Posturepedic cover?

No, you shouldn't unzip or wash a Sealy Posturepedic cover. Nappedia advises never to unzip or wash a Sealy cover. Doing so can disturb the fire sock and void your warranty. The fire sock is the protective layer that holds the flame barrier in place.

Spot clean only, and keep the cover zipped. If your cover gets dirty, blot it with a damp cloth and mild soap, then let it air dry. For deeper cleaning, contact Sealy support or a professional cleaner. You can read our full Sealy brand review for more care tips.

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass in a Sealy Posturepedic bed?

Stop disturbing the bed right away and check for tiny shimmering fibers in a dark room with a phone flashlight before touching or vacuuming anything.

Skin, Eye & Breathing Safety (When to See a Doctor)

Rinse exposed skin with cold water only, not hot water or scrubbing. Hot water opens your pores and traps fibers deeper. See a doctor if you get a lingering cough, wheezing, eye pain, or a rash that won't clear after rinsing.

Spotted a fiberglass leak? File a public safety report at U.S. CPSC SaferProducts.gov →.

Step 1: Shut off fans and HVAC, close the door, and take photos

Turn off ceiling fans and central air right away so fibers don't spread into ducts. Take clear photos of the law tag and shiny dust before you touch the bed.

Step 2: Put on gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection

Wear long sleeves, nitrile gloves, a fitted N95 mask, and glasses before touching anything. That keeps fine glass fibers off your skin, eyes, and airways.

Step 3: Seal and double-bag the mattress before moving it

Don't drag an uncovered bed through hallways. Zip it inside a heavy waterproof vinyl encasement, tape the zipper shut, and double-bag it before disposal.

Step 4: Vacuum slowly with a sealed True HEPA vacuum only

Don't use a regular household vacuum, because it'll blow glass fibers right back out the exhaust. Use a sealed True HEPA vacuum on carpets, baseboards, and frames.

Step 5: Wet-wipe hard surfaces and replace HVAC air filters

Dry dusting pushes fibers back into the air. Wipe floors and nightstands with damp disposable cloths, then swap in fresh high-MERV HVAC filters.

Step 6: Wash lightly exposed clothes alone or toss heavy ones

Bag heavily coated sheets or curtains and throw them out. Rinse lightly exposed clothes outside, wash them alone on cold, and run two empty rinse cycles.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Sealy Posturepedic bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
