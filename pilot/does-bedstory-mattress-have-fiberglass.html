What fire barrier does BedStory use to meet federal rules?

BedStory uses non-glass fire barriers to keep sleepers safe. Every U.S. mattress must pass federal open-flame fire rules under 16 C.F.R. part 1633. That rule uses a 30-minute fire test. Beds must also pass a cigarette test under 16 C.F.R. part 1632.

BedStory pairs its non-glass barriers with CertiPUR-US foams. These foams have no heavy metals or harsh chemicals. Visit our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub to learn how fire barriers work.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party BedStory models differ?

BedStory says on its official FAQ page that all of its beds and toppers are fiberglass-free. Still, old stock from other sellers can sit in warehouses for years. Cheap boxed beds under $500 used fiberglass in the past. So you should always check the law tag when you buy from any third-party site.

Federal rules say every bed must have a sewn-in tag. It shows the month, year, and plant where the bed was made. Reading that tag lets you check what is inside your bed.

What exact language appears on a BedStory law tag?

We have not taken photos of every BedStory law tag. Still, federal law says every bed must have a white law tag. It lists the materials, make date, and plant. If a pad uses chemical flame blockers, it must show the letter T and care steps.

Follow these 4 steps when your BedStory bed arrives:

  1. Find the sewn-in white tag at the head or foot of the bed.
  2. Read the material list to make sure it shows foams and non-glass fibers, not glass fiber.
  3. Check the make date to see when your bed was built.
  4. Look for care rules before you try to take off or wash the cover.

Should you unzip or wash the BedStory mattress cover?

You should never unzip or wash the outer cover of your BedStory bed unless the law tag says it is safe. Even without fiberglass, taking off a tight knit cover can harm the inner fire barrier. It can also ruin the fit. If you ever see an inner fire sock, do not unzip it. Use a washable mattress pad instead to keep your bed clean.

Safe to do
  • Use a washable mattress pad to block spills and stains.
  • Spot clean the outer knit cover with mild soap and water.
  • Check the white law tag for care rules before cleaning.
Never do
  • Never unzip the outer cover unless the law tag says you can.
  • Never machine wash or dry the cover if the tag says spot clean only.
  • Never cut, tear, or open any inner fire-barrier sock.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab the spot lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild dish soap. Never soak the fabric or scrub through the top quilt into the fire-barrier layer underneath.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Leave the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until fully dry. Machine-washing or tumble-drying a structural mattress cover can shrink it and expose the inner barrier.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the intact cover so sweat, spills, and dust mites never reach the mattress fabric in the first place.

Has BedStory had any CPSC recalls or fiberglass issues?

BedStory has zero product recalls on the CPSC list as of November 2026. A check of the CPSC list shows zero recalls for BedStory beds, toppers, or pillows.

BedStory also has zero major federal class-action lawsuits. Still, the brand has a BBB F rating due to buyer issues with returns and warranties.

How do you check an older or secondhand BedStory mattress?

If you buy a used or older BedStory bed, do a quick check first. Follow these easy steps:

  • Check the make date on the white law tag to see how old the bed is.
  • Read the fiber list to make sure it has no glass fiber.
  • Look at the outer cover to be sure it is zipped shut and not torn or washed.
Current models are fiberglass-free. For general fiberglass cleanup steps on older secondhand beds, see our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub.
