What fire barrier does Foreverbed use to meet federal rules?

Foreverbed uses natural Malpaca alpaca wool in its Origin line to pass U.S. fire rules without fiberglass. Every bed sold in the U.S. must pass 16 CFR Part 1632 and 16 CFR Part 1633 fire tests.

In the Onyx line, Foreverbed uses mica-infused cool fabric, Serene foam, and zoned coils. The brand says all of these parts are free of fiberglass and formaldehyde. You can read the CPSC mattress rules to see how U.S. fire tests work.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Foreverbed models differ?

We don't have records showing Foreverbed sells different builds on Amazon, Costco, or other outside stores. Foreverbed designs and builds its beds in Webb City, Missouri, using U.S.-made parts.

If you buy a Foreverbed from an outside seller, don't assume it's the same bed. Ask the seller for the exact model name and check the sewn-in tag first. You can also compare other safe beds in our Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub.

What exact language appears on a Foreverbed law tag?

We haven't seen every Foreverbed law tag in person, so we can't quote the exact text. U.S. rules say the tag must show the build month, build year, and plant site. They don't make every brand list the fire barrier or cover fibers on that tag.

  1. Find the sewn-in white law tag at the head or foot of your Foreverbed.
  2. Check the build date and plant site to see when and where it was made.
  3. Read the fill list for terms like alpaca wool, cotton, or foam.
  4. Ask Foreverbed in writing if the tag doesn't name the fire barrier clearly.

Should you unzip or wash the Foreverbed mattress cover?

You shouldn't unzip or machine-wash a Foreverbed cover unless the care tag says you can. Foreverbed uses hand-tufting and breathable covers made from Belgian damask or mica-infused fabric.

Pulling off a fixed cover can rip the seams and void your warranty. Even on beds with no fiberglass, you shouldn't touch an inner fire layer. Spot-clean spills gently with mild soap and water instead.

Safe to do
  • Use a breathable waterproof bed pad to keep spills off the cover.
  • Spot-clean small stains gently with a damp cloth and mild soap.
  • Vacuum the sleep surface lightly with a clean brush tool.
Never do
  • Never unzip or pull off a tufted or fixed Foreverbed outer cover.
  • Never machine-wash or dry-clean a bed cover unless the tag allows it.
  • Never cut or pull at an inner fire layer or loose tufts.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

How do you check an older or secondhand Foreverbed mattress?

You can check a used Foreverbed by looking at the law tag, seams, and cover before you bring it home. Even though Foreverbed says its beds are fiberglass-free, old used beds can have wear or missing tags.

  • Check the sewn-in law tag for the build month, year, and plant site.
  • Read the fill list for natural fibers like alpaca wool or standard foam.
  • Look over the outer cover for rips, broken tufts, or loose threads.
  • Skip any used bed if the law tag is cut off or hard to read.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Foreverbed bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.

QDoes Foreverbed use fiberglass in any of its mattresses?

No, Foreverbed says all of its parts are CertiPUR-US certified and free of fiberglass and formaldehyde. Both the Origin and Onyx lines are built without fiberglass.

QWhat fire barrier does the Foreverbed Origin mattress use?

The Origin line uses Malpaca alpaca wool for natural fire and dust-mite safety. This helps the bed pass U.S. fire rules without fiberglass.

QAre Foreverbed foams CertiPUR-US certified?

Yes, Foreverbed says its foams are CertiPUR-US certified and free of fiberglass and formaldehyde. Still, the brand doesn't post cert IDs or full lab sheets on its site.

QWhere are Foreverbed mattresses made?

Foreverbed beds are designed and built by hand in Webb City, Missouri. The brand uses U.S.-made parts across its builds.

QDoes the Foreverbed Onyx Alpine use memory foam?

No, the Onyx Alpine bed uses Serene open-cell foam instead of classic memory foam. It also has a cover woven with fine mica-infused fibers for airflow.

QCan I verify a Foreverbed mattress using its law tag?

Yes, you can check the sewn-in law tag for the build date and plant site. Because U.S. rules don't make every tag list the fire barrier, you should also ask Foreverbed in writing.
