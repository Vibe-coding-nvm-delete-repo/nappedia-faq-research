What fire barrier does Helix Midnight use today vs. older models?

Today's Helix Midnight uses a rayon fire barrier, not fiberglass. Rayon is a plant-based fiber that resists flame without glass. Helix pairs it with certified foams to meet federal rules.

Every U.S. mattress must pass open-flame tests under 16 CFR Part 1633. That test runs 30 minutes and measures how big a fire the mattress creates. There is also a second federal standard, 16 CFR Part 1632, which covers smoldering from a lit cigarette. Helix's rayon barrier helps the Midnight pass these tests without glass fibers.

Older or pre-transition Helix models should be checked by law tag. If you bought secondhand or own an early build, look at the tag before you assume. Cheap imports sometimes hide glass fibers inside inner socks, unlike Helix. A quick tag check settles the question for any older bed.

What exact language appears on a Helix Midnight law tag?

A current Helix Midnight law tag lists Rayon, Cellulose, and Polyester, not Glass Fiber. Federal rules require each mattress to carry a permanent label with the month and year of manufacture and the maker's location. Helix's tag should show a recent build date and fiber terms like rayon or cellulose.

Watch for these terms on the tag:

  • Rayon or cellulose: expected on current Helix beds.
  • Polyester: common in cover and comfort layers.
  • Glass fiber or fiberglass: a red flag on any tag.
  • Letter "T": means a chemical flame retardant was added.

Use this 4-step check:

  1. Find the law tag at the foot of the mattress.
  2. Read the manufacture month and year.
  3. Scan the fiber list for glass fiber.
  4. Compare the date to Helix's fiberglass-free production window.

Federal recordkeeping rules also require makers to document any flame retardant treatments and flame resistant fibers used in components. That paper trail helps confirm what is inside.

Should you unzip or wash the Helix Midnight cover?

No, do not unzip or machine-wash the Helix Midnight cover. Helix covers are spot-clean only and should not be unzipped or machine-washed. Keeping the cover sealed protects the fire barrier and the foam inside.

Helix also recommends a breathable waterproof protector from night one. A protector catches spills so the cover stays clean. Spot clean small marks with a damp cloth and mild soap. Let the area air dry fully before you put bedding back on.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab the spot lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild dish soap. Never soak the fabric or scrub through the top quilt into the fire-barrier layer underneath.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Leave the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until fully dry. Machine-washing or tumble-drying a structural mattress cover can shrink it and expose the inner barrier.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the intact cover so sweat, spills, and dust mites never reach the mattress fabric in the first place.

How do you tell if an older Helix Midnight mattress has fiberglass?

Check the law tag first, because it is the fastest way to tell. Current Helix beds are fiberglass-free, but older or secondhand units deserve a look. Use these steps:

  • Find the manufacture date: the law tag shows month and year.
  • Read the fiber list: look for rayon or cellulose, and watch for glass fiber.
  • Check for a "T" label: it signals a chemical flame retardant.
  • Inspect the cover seam: do not unzip it, since Helix covers are spot-clean only.
  • Look for loose shiny fibers: glass fibers can shed near seams or the base.
  • Ask the seller for the tag photo: skip any bed with no tag.

If the tag is missing or unclear, treat the bed as unknown. Cheap imports sometimes hide glass fibers inside inner socks. When in doubt, contact Helix support with the tag photo. You can also browse the Mattress Fiberglass Safety Hub for more checks.
