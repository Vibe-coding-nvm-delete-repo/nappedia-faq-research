What fire barrier does Molecule use to meet federal rules?

Molecule uses a fire-safe fabric sock made from a special blend of fibers. This barrier wraps the whole bed and slows down fire without added chemical sprays. That design helps the bed pass federal open-flame rules under 16 C.F.R. part 1633 and smolder rules under 16 C.F.R. part 1632. You can read the CPSC mattress flammability FAQ to see how those 30-minute flame and smoke tests work.

Molecule's foams are made in the USA and carry the CertiPUR-US label. That means the foams have no heavy metals, ozone depleters, or banned phthalates. Still, Molecule doesn't list organic labels like GOTS or OEKO-TEX, and it doesn't name the exact fibers in its sock. If you've got strong fiber or chemical allergies, ask Molecule's support team for the exact fiber list before you buy.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Molecule models differ?

Third-party or older clearance Molecule beds can have different build dates or factory tags than a bed bought straight from Molecule. Molecule doesn't post a model-by-model fiber list for every third-party site. Because of that, you shouldn't assume a clearance or store listing matches the current direct bed without checking.

Before you unbox a third-party Molecule bed, check the sewn-in law tag for the month, year, and factory site. Then reach out to Molecule with that tag info to confirm in writing that your exact bed is fiberglass-free.

What exact language appears on a Molecule law tag?

A Molecule law tag shows the build date, factory site, and compliance label, though we haven't photographed every Molecule tag in-house. Some brands list glass fiber on the filling line while others don't, so always confirm with Molecule in writing too.

Here's a 4-step check you can run on any Molecule law tag:

  1. Find the white law tag sewn into the head or foot seam.
  2. Read the month, year, and factory site to see when and where the bed was made.
  3. Scan the filling and cover lines for any mention of glass fiber, silica, or modacrylic.
  4. Email Molecule's support team with your tag photo if the fiber list looks unclear.

Should you unzip or wash the Molecule mattress cover?

No, you shouldn't open the main zipper or take off the main cover on a Molecule bed. Opening the main zipper or taking off the cover voids your 15-year warranty, unless your model has a separate zip-off top panel. Keeping the outer cover zipped also protects the inner fire sock from tears or wear. Check Molecule's FAQ page or your care tag if you need spot-cleaning tips.

Safe to do
  • Use a breathable waterproof mattress protector over the cover
  • Spot-clean spills gently with mild soap and cold water
  • Keep the main outer zipper closed at all times
Never do
  • Never unzip or remove the main Molecule mattress cover
  • Never machine-wash or dry-clean the main cover
  • Never cut or open the inner fire-safe sock

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

How do you check an older or secondhand Molecule mattress?

You can check an older or used Molecule bed by looking at its law tag, cover, and seams before bringing it home. Used beds don't carry the 15-year warranty, and older builds may have worn covers.

  • Check the white law tag for the build month, year, and factory site.
  • Check the outer cover and main zipper to make sure nobody has opened or torn the cover.
  • Look for any loose fibers along the seams, and ask Molecule to check the build date in writing.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Molecule bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
