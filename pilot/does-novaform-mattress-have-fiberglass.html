What fire barrier does Novaform use instead of fiberglass?

Novaform meets federal fire rules with a woven fire liner, not glass fiber. To pass those fire rules, FXI puts a woven fire liner under the cover instead of glass fiber. Novaform says its fiberglass-free products use safer fire retardant alternatives.

Federal rule 16 C.F.R. part 1633 tests mattress flammability against an open flame with a 30-minute test. A fire barrier must sit between the foam and the outside world to pass that test.

Novaform foams also carry CertiPUR-US testing. Each foam layer is tested for low VOCs under 0.5 ppm and zero heavy metals or banned chemicals. Nappedia notes that Novaform beds have zero fiberglass and use CertiPUR-US foam.

The liner stays inside the cover. Don't remove it, because it's part of how your bed passes the fire test.

What exact language appears on a Novaform law tag?

A Novaform law tag shouldn't list fiberglass as a fire barrier material. Fiberglass in mattresses is usually woven into a sock-like layer just beneath the outer cover. On a fiberglass-free Novaform, that layer is a woven liner instead.

Use this checklist before you buy or keep a bed:

  1. Find the law tag sewn onto your mattress. You'll need it for warranty claims.
  2. Read the fire barrier or filling lines. Look for words like "fiberglass," "glass fiber," or "fiber glass."
  3. Check the manufacture date. Nappedia advises checking 2023 law tags against Recall #23-793.
  4. Keep the tag and your receipt. Novaform warranty claims require all law tags along with proof of purchase or a packing slip.

No fiberglass wording is the goal. If the tag is missing or unclear, ask the seller before you buy.

How does CPSC Recall #23-793 relate to Novaform fiberglass concerns?

Recall #23-793 is a mold recall, not a fiberglass recall. Novaform has one major CPSC recall: Recall #23-793 from September 2023 covering about 48,000 ComfortGrande 14" and DreamAway 8" beds. The CPSC and FXI issued Recall #23-793 on September 21, 2023 for mold risk.

The recall followed over 500 mold reports after water got onto foam at FXI's San Bernardino, California plant. Recalled item numbers include ComfortGrande 14" King #1413200, Cal King #1413201, Queen #1413202, Full #1413203, and Twin #1413204. They also include DreamAway 8" Twin #1698562 and Full #1698564. You can call FXI at 888-886-2057 for a free replacement or full refund.

Novaform's legal record centers on CPSC Recall #23-793 and follow-up mold investigations rather than a fiberglass class action. Because Novaform uses fiberglass-free liners, it's avoided the fiberglass lawsuits seen by some budget boxed brands.

Mold and fiberglass are separate issues. Don't mix them up when you research a bed.

Should you unzip or wash a Novaform mattress cover?

No, don't unzip or wash a Novaform mattress cover. Nappedia warns never to unzip or wash the outer cover, since that can harm the fire liner and void your warranty. Fiberglass release problems begin when someone unzips and removes the outer cover.

Spot clean the surface with a damp cloth and mild soap. Use a mattress protector to keep your cover clean. Air out your room if the bed has a new-foam smell, which fades in 24 to 72 hours with an open window.

Leave the inner sock alone. The fire barrier isn't a washable part.

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

Do Novaform mattress toppers and pillows contain fiberglass?

Novaform toppers are fiberglass-free, and the federal fire rule treats them differently from full mattresses. Novaform states its mattresses and toppers contain zero fiberglass. That same fiberglass-free claim for mattresses and toppers appears on Novaform's Our Story page.

Federal rule 16 C.F.R. part 1633 covers mattress flammability when exposed to an open flame and prescribes a 30-minute test. That rule targets full mattresses and mattress sets, not every pillow or thin topper. So a topper may not need the same fire barrier sock.

To check a topper or pillow, read the sewn-in tag and the online listing. Look for fiberglass wording and confirm the brand name. Nue by Novaform reportedly doesn't contain fiberglass either.

What should you do if you suspect fiberglass in a Novaform bed?

Stop disturbing the bed right away and check for tiny shimmering fibers in a dark room with a phone flashlight before touching or vacuuming anything.

Skin, Eye & Breathing Safety (When to See a Doctor)

Rinse exposed skin with cold water only, not hot water or scrubbing. Hot water opens your pores and traps fibers deeper. See a doctor if you get a lingering cough, wheezing, eye pain, or a rash that won't clear after rinsing.

Spotted a fiberglass leak? File a public safety report at U.S. CPSC SaferProducts.gov →.

Step 1: Shut off fans and HVAC, close the door, and take photos

Turn off ceiling fans and central air right away so fibers don't spread into ducts. Take clear photos of the law tag and shiny dust before you touch the bed.

Step 2: Put on gloves, an N95 mask, and eye protection

Wear long sleeves, nitrile gloves, a fitted N95 mask, and glasses before touching anything. That keeps fine glass fibers off your skin, eyes, and airways.

Step 3: Seal and double-bag the mattress before moving it

Don't drag an uncovered bed through hallways. Zip it inside a heavy waterproof vinyl encasement, tape the zipper shut, and double-bag it before disposal.

Step 4: Vacuum slowly with a sealed True HEPA vacuum only

Don't use a regular household vacuum, because it'll blow glass fibers right back out the exhaust. Use a sealed True HEPA vacuum on carpets, baseboards, and frames.

Step 5: Wet-wipe hard surfaces and replace HVAC air filters

Dry dusting pushes fibers back into the air. Wipe floors and nightstands with damp disposable cloths, then swap in fresh high-MERV HVAC filters.

Step 6: Wash lightly exposed clothes alone or toss heavy ones

Bag heavily coated sheets or curtains and throw them out. Rinse lightly exposed clothes outside, wash them alone on cold, and run two empty rinse cycles.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Novaform bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
