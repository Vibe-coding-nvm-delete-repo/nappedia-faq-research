What fire barrier does Evergreen Sleep use to meet federal rules?

Evergreen Sleep uses organic wool or a fire-retardant sock under cotton to meet federal rules without fiberglass. Every bed sold in the U.S. must pass CPSC fire rules. Beds must pass cigarette tests under 16 C.F.R. part 1632 and open flame tests under 16 C.F.R. part 1633.

The Evergreen Sleep 100 line uses an organic Joma wool cover to block flames. The Sage and Aspen models also include organic wool comfort layers. The ecoRest line places a fire-retardant sock under its cotton cover. You can read our full Evergreen Sleep brand review to see how these layers feel.

Do Amazon, Costco, or third-party Evergreen Sleep models differ?

Third-party Evergreen Sleep beds don't show any known fiberglass builds. Still, you should always check the exact model name before you buy. Some stores carry older lines like ecoRest or newer models like Sage, Terra, Aspen, or Solis.

Evergreen Sleep posts GOLS, Oeko-Tex 100, and CertiPUR-US marks without certificate numbers or scans. Because of that, it's smart to confirm the exact build with the seller. Ask the store or Evergreen Sleep for the fiber list on your exact model.

What exact language appears on an Evergreen Sleep law tag?

An Evergreen Sleep law tag won't always list the fire barrier or cover fibers. We haven't checked every individual tag in person. Under federal labeling rules, each bed must carry a label with its build month, build year, and plant code. Some brands list glass fiber on the filling line, while others don't. Always confirm with Evergreen Sleep in writing too.

  1. Look at the head or foot of the bed for the white sewn-in law tag.
  2. Check the build date and plant code on the label.
  3. Read the filling lines for wool, latex, cotton, or foam.
  4. Email Evergreen Sleep with your model name and tag date if anything looks unclear.

Should you unzip or wash the Evergreen Sleep mattress cover?

You shouldn't unzip or remove the inner fire-retardant sock on an Evergreen Sleep bed, even though it's fiberglass-free. On models like the ecoRest, this sock sits right under the outer cotton cover to meet U.S. fire rules.

If you spill something, spot-clean the outer fabric gently instead of taking the cover off. Use a waterproof protector to keep your bed clean without touching the inner layers.

Safe to do
  • Use a breathable waterproof mattress protector to prevent spills.
  • Spot-clean the outer cotton or wool cover with mild soap and water.
  • Vacuum the surface gently with an upholstery attachment.
Never do
  • Never unzip or remove an inner fire-retardant sock.
  • Never machine-wash or dry-clean the outer mattress cover unless the tag allows it.
  • Never use harsh bleach or heavy steam on natural wool or latex layers.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

How do you check an older or secondhand Evergreen Sleep mattress?

You can check an older or used Evergreen Sleep bed by looking at its law tag and outer cover. Even though lines like the 100 and ecoRest are fiberglass-free, used beds can have wear or missing tags.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Evergreen Sleep bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.

QDoes the Evergreen Sleep Sage or Aspen mattress have fiberglass?

No, the Evergreen Sleep Sage and Aspen don't use fiberglass. Both models use organic wool and latex over pocket coils.

QWhat does the Evergreen Sleep ecoRest use instead of fiberglass?

The Evergreen Sleep ecoRest line uses a fire-retardant sock under its cotton cover to meet U.S. rules. It also pairs organic latex with gel foam and bio foam.

QAre Evergreen Sleep certifications verified?

Evergreen Sleep claims GOLS, Oeko-Tex 100, Eco Institut, and CertiPUR-US on its site. However, the brand posts no certificate numbers or scans, so those claims aren't verified.

QHas Evergreen Sleep ever had a mattress recall?

No, Evergreen Sleep has zero recalls in the U.S. CPSC recall database as of Nov 23, 2026. A direct check of the CPSC Recall API also returned 0 recalls.

QDoes the Evergreen Sleep Terra or Solis contain fiberglass?

No, neither the Terra nor the Solis contains fiberglass. Both models pair organic latex with an open-cell Bio Foam core.

QWill the Evergreen Sleep law tag list the fire barrier?

Not always, because federal rules only require the build date and plant code on the label. If your tag doesn't list the barrier, email Evergreen Sleep to confirm in writing.
