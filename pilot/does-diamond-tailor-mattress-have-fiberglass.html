What exact language appears on a Diamond Tailor law tag?

We haven't photographed every individual Diamond Tailor law tag in-house, so you should check your own tag. Federal rules under 16 C.F.R. part 1632 require the tag to show the build month, year, and plant location. They don't require every brand to list the fire barrier or cover fibers on that tag. Some makers list glass fiber on the filling line, while others don't, so always confirm in writing too.

  1. Flip your mattress or check the head seam to find the white sewn-in law tag.
  2. Read the filling materials section to see if it lists polyurethane foam, memory foam, or cotton.
  3. Scan the tag for words like glass fiber, fiberglass, or textile glass, which shouldn't appear here.
  4. Write down the manufacture date and plant code in case you need to contact Diamond Mattress.

Should you unzip or wash the Diamond Tailor mattress cover?

You shouldn't unzip or machine-wash the Diamond Tailor outer cover unless the care tag says it's removable. Even though the bed is 100% fiberglass-free, you still want to keep the inner layers intact. If your mattress has an inner fire-barrier sock, never unzip or cut into it. Spot-clean spills gently with mild soap and water instead. You'll keep your warranty safe and your bed clean without risking fabric damage.

Safe to do
  • Use a breathable waterproof mattress protector to stop spills before they reach the cover.
  • Spot-clean small stains with mild soap, cold water, and a damp cloth.
  • Vacuum the top surface gently with a soft brush attachment to clear dust.
Never do
  • Never unzip or remove the outer cover unless the sewn-in care label says it's washable.
  • Never machine-wash or tumble-dry a mattress cover that isn't made for laundering.
  • Never cut, pull, or open any inner fire-barrier sock beneath the outer fabric.

Step 1: Blot spills gently with mild soap and cold water

Dab stains lightly with a damp microfiber cloth and mild soap. Don't soak the cover or scrub down into the inner fire barrier.

Step 2: Air-dry the mattress flat without unzipping the cover

Keep the cover zipped and point a fan across the damp spot until it's dry. Washing a non-removable cover can shrink it and expose the inner sock.

Step 3: Add a breathable waterproof mattress protector

Put a washable waterproof protector over the cover on day one. It'll block sweat and spills before they ever reach the bed.

You can check an older or secondhand Diamond Tailor by inspecting its sewn-in law tag and outer cover. Even though Diamond Mattress states all of its beds are fiberglass-free, it's smart to verify secondhand gear.

Mattress fiberglass terms explained in plain English

Confused by terms like “fire sock,” “silica,” “URN,” or “CertiPUR-US” while checking your Diamond Tailor bed? Here's what each safety term on a tag or product page actually means.

Inner fire sock (FR sock)

It's a thin, stretchy mesh sleeve wrapped tightly around the foam core under the outer cover. It slows open flames, and it can shed glass shards if you unzip or tear the cover.

Assembly zipper vs. washable cover

A zipper on the bottom usually exists for factory assembly, not washing. Don't unzip it unless the care tag clearly says the outer cover is washable.

Glass fiber and core-spun glass

These are common tag names for fiberglass. Treat glass fiber, fiberglass, glass wool, core-spun glass, and glass yarn as the same woven glass material.

Silica-treated rayon vs. fiberglass

Silica-infused rayon chars safely like ash and doesn't shed sharp glass needles. Seeing silica-treated rayon alone isn't the same as woven fiberglass.

CertiPUR-US doesn't mean fiberglass-free

CertiPUR-US only tests the inner foam for VOCs and chemicals, never the fire sock. Only finished-product GOTS or GOLS organic standards ban fiberglass socks.

6-sided encasement vs. fitted protector

A fitted protector leaves the bottom seam open. Use a 6-sided zippered waterproof encasement if you need to seal a suspect bed completely.

URN and manufacture date

The Uniform Registry Number (like CA-XXXXX) and build date identify the factory and year. You'll need that date to check older pre-transition stock.

16 CFR Part 1632 vs. 1633

Every U.S. bed must pass both federal fire tests. Part 1632 tests smoldering cigarettes, while Part 1633 tests a 30-minute open flame.
